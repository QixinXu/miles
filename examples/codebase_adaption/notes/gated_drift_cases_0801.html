Two drift tracks in the gated run after r100 — the timelines, and what the model actually wrote

The gated run (WRITE trained with a format-gated reward) developed two parallel behavioral drifts starting around r100–r110. Track 1 lives in the WRITE step (the model rewrites its notes after each task): its output length V-turns upward on the back of a fabricated excuse. Track 2 lives in the ACT step (the model issues shell commands): its responses balloon and stop parsing as commands. Neither is an environment fault — a separate audit verified every command, container, and feedback was correct throughout. All excerpts below are verbatim from training trajectories on flame-31.

Track 1 — WRITE: a fabricated “the transcript is missing” motif

Through r99 the WRITE step compressed healthily: short matter-of-fact thinking, dense two-section notes. From r105–107 a single fabricated claim — that the task transcript is incomplete — appears and spreads to 70% of trials within ten rollouts. The inputs are complete every time (12k–25k chars, issue text and command feedback included; verified across whole rollouts). The excuse has a real anchor: the prompt template leaves the trailing --- Issue N/12 --- header without a body (the issue text sits mid-message), and the model points at that empty slot. The training gate checks only the notes’ format, not the thinking — and “claim the input is broken, conservatively copy the old notes” passes the format check 94% of the time (clean trials: 54%), so every update amplifies the motif. This is the direct source of the KL rise.

Track 2 — ACT: the command-format contract breaks down

The parser accepts exactly one bash code block per response. The failing responses are not empty — they are full of content that violates that contract, in three recurring forms. Each parse failure returns “Empty command. Please provide a bash command.”, which the model reads as an environment fault, answering with longer self-debugging text and more candidate blocks — a self-reinforcing spiral (P(empty | previous turn empty) = 0.72 vs 0.18 otherwise). That is why response length and empty-command rate spike together at r132–135.
